The Front Page

Local designer ships things with weight

Interfaces reported to roll, fold, settle and pop; sources confirm nothing he makes will simply fade in.

By The Sisodia Times StaffZop.Dev Bureau

ZOP.DEV HQ — A designer here has been observed, for the third consecutive year, refusing to ship anything that does not feel like a physical object. Ravindra Sisodia, who works on brand and product at cloud platform Zop.Dev, reportedly believes software should have mass, momentum and consequences.

Witnesses describe shop shutters that roll up when his laptop unlocks, pages that turn with visible paper stress, and a bubble-wrap application with audible pops. “He kept saying the word ‘thunk’ in a design review,” said one colleague. “We now all say it.”

“If you can’t imagine the sound it makes, it isn’t finished.”— R. Sisodia, allegedly

The designer’s output spans brand systems, motion, WebGL toys and a growing settlement of small macOS creatures. Asked for comment, Mr. Sisodia opened another window instead.

Analysts warn the behaviour is likely to continue. “There is no cure,” one said. “There is only more shipping.”
