B. Product, macOS

Crew, a tiny crew of pixel monsters

One creature per Claude Code chat and subagent, living along the bottom of your screen. Free for macOS, MIT licensed.

  • RoleConcept, design, code, art.
  • PlatformmacOS 13 or newer.
  • LicenceMIT. Free, with a 12 dollar one time Pro tier.
  • Year2026

A glance tells you which agent is working, which is stuck waiting on you, and which finished ten minutes ago.

You can run four or five Claude Code sessions at once and all of it is invisible. Reading a dashboard costs a glance, a parse and a context switch, which is three costs for one piece of information.

So the state is a creature instead. You notice something is wrong from the corner of your eye, before you have consciously looked at anything.

The rule the whole thing is built on: it never invents a state. Every animation is driven by a real hook event.

  • 10Species drawn in code

  • 0Servers, accounts or telemetry

Plate iii. Six of the ten species. Each has walk, blink and sleep states, all driven by hook events rather than a timer.

C. Biography

Hard light, flat colour

Where the taste came from, and what happens on the weekends.

  • BornJodhpur, Rajasthan.
  • Works fromBengaluru, India, UTC+5:30.
  • ExperienceFive years across B2B SaaS, fintech and enterprise.
  • Off hoursMotorbikes, mostly.

Jodhpur is a city of one colour and very little shade. Blue walls, ochre stone, a sun that flattens everything it touches into shapes. Growing up inside that is a design education you do not notice you are getting, and it is still the reason his work leans on hard edges, high contrast and colour used flat rather than blended.

The rest of it is method. Design the thing properly in Figma, then build it properly in Claude Code, then keep going until it runs on a real machine. Five years of B2B SaaS, fintech and enterprise taught him where interfaces actually break, which is rarely in the pretty screen and usually in the fourth state nobody drew.

When a build is thinking, he rides. It is the only reliable way he has found to stop looking at a problem long enough to solve it.

Pixels by day, petrol by dusk. Ravindra Sisodia
